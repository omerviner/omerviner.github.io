The product

OrCam Hear is a pair of AI earbuds for people with mild hearing loss. It isolates the voices you choose and suppresses background noise in real time. Users control it from an iPhone app in the middle of a conversation, often at a busy table. So the app has to work at a glance, and without making a show of managing a hearing problem.

The design I built

The UX lead, Rony Dagan, and the UI designer, Sarai Beris, designed the app around a spatial model. The room holds the people you're listening to. The balcony holds people you've enrolled who are waiting. Strangers stay outside. You pull people into the conversation or push them out with one quick move. My job was to turn that model into a smooth, reliable iOS app connected to the earbuds.

What I did on the app

  • Figma to Swift. Implemented the designs screen by screen and kept them pixel-accurate, working directly with the designers on motion and edge cases.
  • Component library in code. Built every UI component in Swift, matching the designers' component library, so new screens were assembled from shared parts.
  • Audio-driven animation. Animations react to live audio from the device, so users can see who is being heard and that the earbuds are working.
  • Smooth interaction. Fast, predictable gestures for moving people between the room and the balcony, tuned for use while talking.
  • State and data. Managed the app's state and linked the UI to the app's data layer, which connects to the embedded device, including enrolled voices, who is active and device status.

On the device

  • Developed real-time C++ software for the embedded device: multi-process, multi-threaded, under tight compute and power limits.
  • Owned parts of the real-time audio-video flow: components that capture, process and deliver sound and image data.
  • Integrated ML algorithms; built performance optimizations, usage analytics and internal tools.
  • Coordinated development and testing with vision, DSP, algorithms, embedded, mobile and QA teams.
